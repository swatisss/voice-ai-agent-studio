## When to call 999 or go to A&E

Call 999 or go to A&E straight away, not the dental practice, if you have swelling that makes it hard to breathe or swallow, swelling that is spreading to your eye or down your neck, bleeding from the mouth that does not stop after 20 minutes of firm pressure, or a dental injury together with a head injury, such as being knocked out or being sick.

## Same-day emergency appointments

Every practice keeps emergency appointments free each day for patients in pain. Call your practice from 8am to get one; they are given in the order people call. An emergency appointment costs £85 and covers the assessment and temporary relief of pain. New patients can book an emergency appointment too.

## Knocked-out adult tooth

Find the tooth and hold it by the crown (the white part), not the root. If it is dirty, rinse it briefly in milk or cold water; do not scrub it. If you can, push it gently back into the socket and bite on a clean cloth. If not, keep it in milk or inside your cheek. Come to the practice or call the emergency line straight away: the best chance of saving the tooth is within one hour. Never put a baby tooth back in; bring the child to the practice instead.

## Broken tooth, lost filling or lost crown

A chipped or broken tooth, a lost filling or a crown that has come off is not usually an emergency unless it is very painful or has a sharp edge cutting your mouth. Book an appointment within the next few days. Keep a crown that has come off and bring it with you; it can often be put back. A temporary filling kit from a pharmacy can cover a lost filling until your appointment.

## Toothache

Toothache that keeps you awake, toothache with swelling or a bad taste, or toothache that has lasted more than two days needs an emergency appointment. A pharmacist can advise on pain relief while you wait. Our team cannot diagnose a dental problem or recommend medicines over the phone.

## Bleeding after an extraction

Some oozing is normal for a day. Bite firmly on a clean, rolled-up gauze pad or damp tea bag for 20 minutes without checking it. If the bleeding has not slowed after an hour of pressure, call your practice, or the out-of-hours emergency line on 0808 157 0123. Heavy bleeding that will not stop is a reason to go to A&E.

## After a tooth extraction

For the first 24 hours: do not rinse your mouth, smoke, drink alcohol or have hot drinks, and avoid hard exercise. Eat soft food on the other side. After 24 hours, rinse gently with warm salt water after meals for a week. If pain gets worse instead of better two to four days after the extraction, or you notice a bad taste or smell, call the practice: it can be a dry socket, which the dentist can treat.

## After a filling

If you had a local anaesthetic your mouth will be numb for two to four hours. Don't eat until the numbness wears off, to avoid biting your cheek or burning your mouth. Some sensitivity to hot and cold is normal for up to two weeks. If your bite feels high, or the tooth still hurts after two weeks, call the practice to have it checked; there is no charge for this.

## After root canal treatment

The tooth may be tender for a few days. Avoid chewing hard food on it until the permanent filling or crown is fitted. Call the practice if you have swelling, or pain that gets worse after three days.

## After teeth whitening

Teeth can be sensitive for a few days. For 48 hours after each treatment avoid coffee, tea, red wine, curry and smoking, as teeth stain more easily. Stop using the gel and call the practice if your gums become sore or white.

## After a hygiene appointment

Gums may bleed a little for a day or two and teeth may feel sensitive; this settles quickly. Keep brushing twice a day and clean between your teeth once a day.

## Nervous patients

Tell us when you book if you feel anxious about the dentist. We give nervous patients longer appointments at no extra cost, agree a stop signal before we start, and explain each step before we do it. You can bring someone with you into the surgery, and you can listen to your own music.

## Sedation

Intravenous (IV) sedation helps very anxious patients relax during treatment. It is available at Oakhaven Leeds and Oakhaven Manchester after a free sedation consultation. It costs £295 per appointment on top of the treatment and needs a £50 deposit. You must bring an adult who takes you home and stays with you, and you must not drive, work machinery or sign important documents for 24 hours afterwards. Sedation is not covered by Oakhaven Dental Insurance.

## Making a complaint

We want to put things right. Tell any member of the team, or write to the practice manager at complaints@oakhaven-dental.example. We acknowledge every complaint within 3 working days and send a full response within 20 working days. If you are not happy with our response about private treatment, you can contact the Dental Complaints Service, an independent service for private dental patients.

## Your dental records

You can ask for a copy of your dental records and X-rays at any time. Email the practice or ask at reception; it is free and we send it within one month. We never share your records with anyone else without your consent, except where the law requires it.
