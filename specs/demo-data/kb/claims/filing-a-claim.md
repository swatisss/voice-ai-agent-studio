# Overview

For most health care in the network your provider files the claim for you and you do not need to do anything. File a claim yourself when you paid the provider directly, when you used an out-of-network provider in an emergency, or for a motor policy incident such as a broken windscreen or a collision.

## What we ask for in a first notice

A first notice of claim records the claim so the claims team can start. Have these ready: the policy the claim is for, what kind of claim it is, the date of the service or incident, the provider or garage, a short description, and a rough amount if you know it. You do not need every document on the day; you can send them afterwards.

## Time limits

File within 90 days of the service or incident. After 90 days the claim can still be filed, but it is reviewed as a late claim and may need an explanation.

## After the first notice

You receive a claim number that starts with C and five digits. Keep it: it is how you check the status by phone or in your online account. A claims specialist contacts you only if something is missing. Most claims are decided within 30 days of receiving all documents.

## Duplicates

A claim for the same policy, date and provider that already exists is not filed again. The agent gives you the existing claim number instead.
